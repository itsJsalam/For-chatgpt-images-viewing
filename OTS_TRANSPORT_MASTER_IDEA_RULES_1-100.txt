1. THE COMPLETE IDEA
====================================================================

OTS Transport / Khuzdar Today Car Service is intended to be a premium, safety-focused intercity transport and seat-booking platform beginning in Khuzdar, Balochistan, Pakistan, and supporting routes across Balochistan and other parts of Pakistan. It is designed around scheduled rides, transparent seat-by-seat fares, vehicle/seat selection, digital tickets, luggage tracking, trip communications, live GPS, and emergency assistance. It should feel like a polished, trustworthy transport product rather than a rough demo.

The service may support cars/sedans, hi-roof vans, Coasters, and buses, with vehicle-specific seating layouts and fares. Routes can include intermediate stops, allowing a seat to be sold for one segment and reused for a later non-overlapping segment of the same trip. For example, a seat may be reserved from Quetta to Khuzdar and then become available for Khuzdar to Karachi, provided the route segments do not overlap and the timing/capacity rules validate.

The starting budget preference is PKR 0 and free-first tools/services. This is a target, not a promise that every production feature can run for free. SMS/OTP, WhatsApp business messaging, maps/GPS usage, hosting, storage, payment gateways, app distribution, and some operational scale may introduce costs or provider limits. Any paid requirement must be identified before commitment; do not buy credits or subscriptions without explicit approval.

2. THE THREE-PART SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
====================================================================

APP 1 — PASSENGER + DRIVER ANDROID APP
One Android app with distinct passenger and driver experiences, clear mode selection, separate authentication/registration flows where needed, and role-based navigation. Passengers search and book; drivers apply, manage approved vehicle/trips, and operate assigned journeys. One driver is registered to one car in the current plan, not multiple cars. That may be reconsidered only in a future change approved by the user.

APP 2 — OPERATIONS AND STAFF ANDROID APP
A separate app for the three operational business owners and their authorized managers/workers. The three operational owners are actual business owners, not ordinary staff. The app supports driver/document review, route and stop management, schedules/fares, bookings, cancellations/refunds, staff management, checkpoint/ticketing work, breakdown response, complaints, and operational audit. Permissions are role-specific.

SILENT OWNER — ONE PRIVATE WEBSITE
A separate, private website for exactly one Silent Owner account. This is not the same as the three operational owners. It provides protected backend/database access, system-wide settings, emergency safety controls, private security alerts, system health, secure backups/restoration, and high-risk administrative actions. The Silent Owner and this website must remain undiscoverable to passengers, drivers, operational owners, managers, workers, and ordinary operational reports/logs. It must not be exposed in either app's navigation, API responses, or routine business reporting.

BACKEND
The likely stack is Supabase/PostgreSQL with server-enforced authorization, authentication, protected storage, and realtime capabilities, subject to a later architecture/security review. The backend—not a hidden button or client-side check—must enforce permissions and emergency freeze rules. Kotlin + Jetpack Compose is the intended Android direction. A Next.js/React-style private website is a possible Silent Owner implementation. These are planning choices, not a statement that production deployment is complete.

3. IMPORTANT CHANGES FROM EARLIER IDEAS
====================================================================

1. Three operational owners are actual business owners, separate from workers and managers.
2. Exactly one separate Silent Owner exists. It is invisible to all operational users and has absolute system authority through a private website.
3. Maintenance mode was moved OUT of the Silent Owner website. Maintenance mode belongs only to the three operational owners' accounts. Any one operational owner can activate, schedule, change, end, or cancel it. Managers/workers can only request or report maintenance.
4. An ordinary operational freeze and the Silent Owner emergency freeze are different. Owners can lift an ordinary freeze; only the Silent Owner can lift its own emergency freeze.
5. One driver is registered to one car in the current design; do not support multiple cars under one driver account for now.
6. A standard car layout has five seats total: one driver seat, one front passenger seat, and three rear passenger seats.
7. Creating/replacing an operational owner requires each of the three existing operational owners' individual OTP approvals. No two-of-three bypass. If any owner is unavailable, the request remains blocked. The Silent Owner can independently override owner administration.
8. Cancellation policy is based on elapsed time after booking confirmation, not time remaining before departure: within the first 8 hours, 100% refund; after the first 8 hours, 75% refund and 25% retained. Exactly 8 hours counts as after the first eight-hour period and receives 75%.
9. Driver cancellation should not harm passengers: attempt a suitable replacement; company bears replacement extra cost; if no suitable replacement exists, passengers receive a full refund.
10. A passenger who misses departure for reasons within their control is a no-show with no automatic refund; company-caused delays or incorrect information receive protection and may require replacement/full refund.
11. English and Urdu are required across both apps and the Silent Owner website where appropriate, with correct Urdu right-to-left layout.
12. First-launch language choice is shown once, remembered, and changeable in Settings.
13. Rule 93 added app-update behavior; Rule 94 added online/offline/reconnecting behavior; Rule 95 added notification permissions/fallbacks; Rule 96 added session/device security; Rule 97 added privacy/data access; Rule 98 added fraud monitoring; Rule 99 added support/complaints; Rule 100 opened a recommendation for a full requirements audit before further implementation.
14. The user wants one question at a time and is still in requirements/planning mode. Do not make code changes or claim a feature is implemented unless explicitly authorized.

IMPORTANT NUMBERING NOTE
The following records the user's earlier numbered discussion in its own sequence. The later product specification below also numbers foundational product requirements 1–43, so these are two different numbering systems. Both are retained to avoid losing either the user's decisions or the product requirements. The rules below are reconstructed faithfully from the full discussion pasted by the user on 24 October 2026; they are detailed summaries, not a claim that every sentence is a verbatim transcript.

CONVERSATION RULE 1 — Silent Owner authority and irreversible deletion
OTS Transport has one hidden Silent Owner with ultimate emergency authority. The Silent Owner can trigger a one-tap emergency freeze affecting both the Passenger/Driver app and the Workers/Operational Owners app; override operational owners; control accounts, permissions, system settings and database records; and initiate permanent deletion of the entire database. The Silent Owner works only through a separate private website, not either app. No Silent Owner account, role, entry point or management option appears in ordinary operational interfaces. Emergency freeze is immediate; permanent database destruction is deliberately separated behind a warning, a protected 24-character confirmation key, backend verification and an explicit deletion process. Backup retention/deletion must be explained separately, and database master credentials must never be exposed in browser code.

CONVERSATION RULE 2 — Emergency freeze behavior
The emergency freeze stops new activity: new bookings, new trip publishing and routine operations are blocked. Existing journeys continue to have access to essential trip information and tickets, and SOS/emergency safety paths remain available wherever technically possible. Both apps are affected. The Silent Owner website remains available to investigate and lift the freeze. Freeze enforcement belongs to the backend, not just the app interface.

CONVERSATION RULE 3 — Permanent database deletion flow
The Silent Owner selects “Permanently Delete Entire Database”; the website presents a clear warning describing consequences and backup behavior; the Silent Owner enters a 24-character confirmation key; the backend securely verifies authorization and the key; only then may irreversible deletion begin. The key must be securely stored, never exposed in browser code or logs, and protected against repeated guessing. The policy must say whether live data, backup copies and stored files are included; deleting live data alone does not mean retained backups are destroyed.

CONVERSATION RULE 4 — Silent Owner authentication
The private site requires a unique username and strong password plus an authenticator security code or passkey. Destructive actions also require the separate 24-character confirmation key. Backend-enforced authorization, rate limits, secure sessions and protections against unauthorized access are mandatory. There is no public registration or ordinary “Forgot password?” flow for this account. A separate secure recovery process must be defined so the safety authority cannot be lost permanently.

CONVERSATION RULE 5 — Private provisioning of the single Silent Owner
Exactly one Silent Owner account is privately provisioned during initial setup. There is no public registration or way for operational users to create another. Credentials, passkey/authenticator and destructive-action key are configured privately. The backend enforces the one-account limit; merely hiding a creation screen is not enough. The website remains separate from both apps.

CONVERSATION RULE 6 — Hidden from operational views
Workers and operational owners cannot see or discover the Silent Owner identity, account, role, private website, controls or detailed private audit records in user lists, reports, logs, dashboards or app interfaces. This is enforced by backend authorization and data separation, not only by hiding UI elements. The Silent Owner website retains its protected audit trail. Hiding it from business operators does not mean the private security trail is omitted.

CONVERSATION RULE 7 — Operational owner authority boundary
The three operational owners have broad authority to run the transport business: driver verification, document approval/rejection, route/stop/schedule/vehicle/fare operations, bookings, cancellations/refunds, finances, worker management, terminal work, breakdowns, passenger safety and routine operations. They cannot access, discover, manage, impersonate or override the Silent Owner, its website, credentials, keys or private records. They cannot permanently destroy the entire database. Backend checks enforce the boundary.

CONVERSATION RULE 8 — Operational freeze
Any one of the three operational owners can activate an ordinary operational freeze during an incident. The action is recorded in the operational audit trail. This ordinary freeze is separate from the higher-level Silent Owner emergency freeze. The Silent Owner can override ordinary freezes and retains the ultimate emergency authority.

CONVERSATION RULE 9 — Shared operational unfreeze
Any of the three operational owners can lift an ordinary operational freeze, even if another owner initiated it. No one needs the original initiator to be online. This does not grant the ability to lift a Silent Owner emergency freeze.

CONVERSATION RULE 10 — Safe ordinary freeze behavior
An ordinary operational freeze stops new bookings and new trip publishing. Existing tickets and trip details remain accessible, active journeys can continue, and SOS remains available wherever connectivity permits. Any operational owner can lift this ordinary freeze. The system must distinguish this freeze from the Silent Owner emergency freeze.

CONVERSATION RULE 11 — Emergency freeze precedence
Operational and Silent Owner freezes are tracked separately. Owners can lift operational freezes, but the apps remain restricted while the Silent Owner emergency freeze is active. Only the Silent Owner can lift its emergency freeze. Changes to an ordinary freeze can never accidentally or deliberately override the Silent Owner restriction. Essential active-journey safety and SOS paths remain available wherever possible.

CONVERSATION RULE 12 — Operational audit visibility
All three operational owners can review each other's operational actions, including driver approvals, fare and route changes, refunds and operational freeze events. Workers see only information required for assigned duties. Silent Owner identity, actions and private audit records remain hidden from all operational accounts. The Silent Owner maintains a separate protected audit trail.

CONVERSATION RULE 13 — Owner creation/replacement requires unanimity
Owner-management options may appear in operational owner accounts, but a new operational owner cannot be created or an existing one replaced until all three existing operational owners approve using their individual OTPs. Each OTP must be verified and tied to that specific request. These controls cannot create, replace or change the Silent Owner.

CONVERSATION RULE 13A — Unavailable owner and emergency override
If even one operational owner is unavailable, the owner creation/replacement request remains blocked; the other two cannot bypass the missing approval. The Silent Owner can independently add, remove, replace or freeze an operational owner. OTPs are short-lived, single-use and request-bound.

CONVERSATION RULE 14 — Owner self-service password recovery
Each operational owner can recover their own account using SMS OTP sent to their verified phone number and then set a new password. They do not need the other two owners to reset their own password. This process cannot reset, replace, freeze or alter another owner's account. OTP expiry, one-time use and rate limits are required. The Silent Owner retains independent emergency authority.

CONVERSATION RULE 15 — Owner phone-number changes
An operational owner may change only their own registered phone number after authenticating with their existing account and verifying the new number by SMS OTP. The other owners' approval is not required. If the owner loses both account access and access to the old number, a separate recovery procedure is required. No owner can change another owner's number through this flow.

CONVERSATION RULE 16 — Worker permissions
Workers may sell walk-in/counter tickets and record cash, search bookings by CNIC/phone/ticket ID, print and attach luggage tags, scan tickets, support boarding and luggage handover, and print/export authorized passenger manifests. They cannot approve/reject drivers, change or approve fares, issue refunds, manage owner accounts, freeze/unfreeze apps, or access Silent Owner controls or records. Backend permission checks are mandatory.

CONVERSATION RULE 17 — Offline counter ticketing
Workers may prepare cash ticket sales while offline, but a sale remains pending and is not a confirmed booking until the server verifies seat and route-segment availability after reconnection. Use a secure pending queue, idempotency and duplicate prevention. If the seat has been taken, flag the conflict for staff resolution rather than falsely confirming the ticket. Record cash for reconciliation.

CONVERSATION RULE 18 — Worker cancellation requests
Workers may look up a ticket and submit a cancellation request, but cannot independently finalize cancellations or issue refunds. An operational owner must authorize cancellation and any refund; a manager may do so only if assigned the necessary refund permission. Preserve the original booking and financial history.

CONVERSATION RULE 19 — Routine approval authority
Any one operational owner can approve routine business actions, including driver onboarding, routes/schedules/fares, refunds and cancellation requests, worker management, terminal operations, breakdown response and routine business decisions. The exception is owner creation/replacement, which requires all three owners' OTP approvals. Silent Owner authority remains separate and superior.

CONVERSATION RULE 20 — Owner disagreements and future managers
Routine actions can proceed with one operational owner's authorization and must be recorded in the operational audit history visible to the three owners. The architecture should support managers as a separate future role without rebuilding the platform. Managers do not automatically become owners and cannot access Silent Owner controls or protected records. Exact manager permissions are assigned explicitly.

CONVERSATION RULE 21 — Managers' substantial operational powers
Managers are not limited to observation. If appointed and granted the relevant permissions, they may approve/reject drivers, process eligible refunds, create/manage routes, stops and schedules, make major route/fare changes, supervise staff and terminals, monitor bookings/sales, coordinate trips and breakdowns, and arrange passenger reassignment. Their access is permission-based and never includes owner-account management or overriding a Silent Owner emergency freeze.

CONVERSATION RULE 22 — Manager appointment and permissions
Any one operational owner can create a manager account, assign or change its permissions, grant/refine powers for driver approvals, refunds and route/fare changes, and suspend or remove the manager. Actions are recorded for operational oversight. Managers cannot give themselves powers or access the Silent Owner's private controls.

CONVERSATION RULE 23 — Managers cannot appoint managers
Only the three operational owners can create, remove, suspend or change manager accounts and permissions. Managers cannot appoint other managers, promote staff into management or administer their peers' accounts.

CONVERSATION RULE 24 — Manager-initiated ordinary freeze
An authorized manager can initiate an ordinary operational freeze during a serious incident. The system must notify all three operational owners before or as the freeze is activated. The freeze is logged. Any operational owner can lift it. A Silent Owner emergency freeze remains unliftable by owners/managers.

CONVERSATION RULE 25 — No wait for owner approval
A manager does not have to wait for an owner's response before activating an urgent ordinary freeze. The system sends notifications to all three operational owners and lets the manager proceed immediately. Record the manager, timestamp and reason if provided. Notification failures are recorded and retried where possible but do not block the urgent freeze. Preserve existing journeys and SOS wherever possible.

CONVERSATION RULE 26 — Shared unfreeze of manager-initiated freeze
Any operational owner or any authorized manager may lift an ordinary operational freeze regardless of who initiated it. The action is recorded. Managers cannot lift the Silent Owner emergency freeze.

CONVERSATION RULE 27 — Managers cannot alter each other's permissions
Every manager has an individual account and independently assigned permissions. Manager A cannot change Manager B's permissions; managers cannot self-grant privileges or pass them to another manager. Only operational owners can assign, change or revoke manager permissions. Actions are attributed to the individual manager.

CONVERSATION RULE 28 — Managers can review each other's work
Managers can review operational actions by other managers, including driver approvals, refunds, route/fare changes and freeze events. Records identify the actor and cannot be silently changed. The three operational owners retain oversight. Silent Owner identity, actions and protected audit remain hidden.

CONVERSATION RULE 29 — Authorized reversals between managers
A manager may reverse another manager's decision only if the reversing manager has the relevant permission. Preserve the original action and record the reversal, timestamp and reason. For financial actions, use a compensating adjustment transaction rather than deleting or overwriting the original transaction. Owner oversight remains available.

CONVERSATION RULE 30 — Workers see operational outcomes, not management records
Workers see only task-relevant results and instructions, such as cancellation/refund approval, driver approval status, ticket validity, boarding clearance and route/trip changes affecting their work. They do not see full financial records, management audit histories, unrelated worker information or Silent Owner protected information.

CONVERSATION RULE 31 — Individual worker accounts
Every worker has an individual login, assigned role, terminal/work location and permissions. No shared staff login. Attribute ticket sales, cash records, luggage handling, scans and other actions to the actual worker. Access can be suspended without affecting other accounts; records remain preserved.

CONVERSATION RULE 32 — Worker account administration
Any operational owner can create, edit, suspend or remove workers. A manager can do so only when an owner explicitly grants the relevant worker-management permission. Managers cannot grant themselves or pass along that permission. Record account-management actions for operational oversight.

CONVERSATION RULE 33 — Worker departure and restoration
An operational owner or authorized manager can suspend a departing worker immediately. Suspension blocks sign-in and work, and revokes active sessions where possible. Preserve the worker's ticket-sale, cash, luggage and scan history. An authorized owner can reactivate the worker if they return; manager access depends on assigned permissions.

CONVERSATION RULE 34 — Manager account suspension
Any operational owner can immediately suspend or reactivate a manager. Managers cannot suspend, reactivate or alter peer manager accounts. Suspension revokes access and active sessions where possible while preserving prior actions and audit history. A suspended manager cannot use assigned permissions.

CONVERSATION RULE 35 — Silent Owner can block any account tier
The Silent Owner can immediately block or suspend any operational owner, manager, worker, passenger or driver without the target's approval. The backend enforces the block and revokes active sessions where possible. Records remain intact unless a separate authorized deletion process is used. This ability remains hidden in the private website.

CONVERSATION RULE 36 — Account restoration hierarchy
Workers may be restored by operational owners or managers with permission. Managers may be restored only by operational owners. If the Silent Owner blocked an operational owner, only the Silent Owner can restore that owner. Passenger/driver accounts may be restored by authorized operational owners/managers unless a Silent Owner restriction remains active. Only the Silent Owner can lift its emergency freeze or its own protected restrictions. Ordinary reactivation must never bypass a Silent Owner block.

CONVERSATION RULE 37 — No mandatory reason for Silent Owner account blocks
The Silent Owner can block any account immediately without entering a reason or waiting for approval. The system automatically records basic protected audit facts such as target account, action and timestamp. A reason may be added but is optional. Operational users cannot view this private record.

CONVERSATION RULE 38 — No mandatory reason for Silent Owner emergency freeze
The Silent Owner can activate the emergency freeze with one tap, without a reason or approval. No waiting period applies and operational owners cannot override it. Preserve existing journeys and SOS wherever safely possible. Automatically record the event in the protected audit trail, hidden from operational users.

CONVERSATION RULE 39 — Silent Owner control of operational-owner permissions
The Silent Owner can independently grant, modify or revoke an operational owner's individual permissions, restrict selected capabilities without freezing the whole account, block the entire account, or restore access/permissions. The backend enforces these changes; only the private website exposes the controls.

CONVERSATION RULE 40 — Silent Owner control of manager permissions
The Silent Owner can directly grant, modify, revoke or restore any manager's permissions, restrict specific capabilities, or block the manager's entire account. These powers override operational-owner decisions and are private to the Silent Owner website.

CONVERSATION RULE 41 — Silent Owner financial oversight
The Silent Owner has read access to online payments, transaction history, cash sales and reconciliation, refunds/cancellation fees, driver earnings/payouts, route fares, terminal/toll fees, revenue analytics and discrepancies. It can make authorized corrections and adjustments. Never silently overwrite financial history: preserve the original transaction and record an adjustment. Permanent database deletion remains a separate protected action requiring the 24-character confirmation key.

CONVERSATION RULE 42 — Silent Owner access to personal and identity records
The Silent Owner can access passenger and driver identity details, verified phone numbers, driver CNIC front/back, selfies, licences, vehicle documents, bookings, tickets, route/trip histories, emergency alerts and safety reports. Sensitive documents remain in private storage and are served only through secure authorized backend requests, never public predictable URLs. This access is unavailable to ordinary operational users except for narrow tasks explicitly required by their role.

CONVERSATION RULE 43 — Silent Owner live GPS and trip history
The Silent Owner can view live GPS for active vehicles/trips, route, pickup/drop-off stops, journey status, relevant location history and emergency alert locations. Safety information for active journeys remains available during freezes wherever connectivity permits. GPS collection is limited to defined operational/safety purposes, with clear disclosure, retention limits and access controls.

CONVERSATION RULE 44 — GPS retention
Keep live GPS available during the active trip. Retain ordinary detailed trip GPS history for 90 days, then automatically delete it unless a justified exception applies. Retain SOS/breakdown GPS records for one year and then review whether deletion or a justified extension is needed. Hold investigation-related GPS only as long as genuinely required with periodic review. Retain financial records under applicable legal/accounting requirements. Restrict and protect detailed location access.

CONVERSATION RULE 45 — Silent Owner system-wide configuration
The Silent Owner can change system-wide settings such as refunds, luggage fees, seat-hold duration, routes/fares/taxes, verification rules, notification settings and security/permissions. Changes apply to future bookings by default; existing confirmed prices and terms must not unexpectedly change. Any necessary correction to an existing booking must be authorized, transparent and recorded. The backend remains the authority.

4. FOUNDATION RULES 1–43
====================================================================

RULE 1 — Product purpose and service area
Build OTS Transport as a real intercity transport booking and operations platform, starting from Khuzdar, Balochistan, and expanding across Pakistan routes. The product should prioritize safety, transparency, reliable scheduling, and an understandable passenger journey.

RULE 2 — Premium, usable experience
The app should have a polished, premium interface with readable typography, clear contrast, coherent layouts, quality vehicle/route imagery, and a consistent OTS identity. Visual polish must not come at the expense of accessibility, reliability, or safety.

RULE 3 — Android-first passenger/driver application
The main public app is Android. Passenger and driver workflows coexist but remain distinct. The user should not be trapped in a mode accidentally and should have clear back/navigation controls in every relevant flow.

RULE 4 — Separate operations application
Operational staff and the three operational owners use a separate app from passengers/drivers. Its screens and permissions should be designed for operational tasks, not copied blindly from the public app.

RULE 5 — Silent Owner is a separate private website
Exactly one Silent Owner account uses a separate private website. It is not a fourth operational owner and is not a role users can select or discover. Its protected backend controls must remain inaccessible from the public/operations app.

RULE 6 — Backend is the security authority
Authorization, booking integrity, role checks, emergency freeze enforcement, and sensitive operations must be enforced server-side. Hiding a UI control is not sufficient security.

RULE 7 — Role-based, least-privilege access
Each user sees and performs only the actions needed for their role and explicit permissions. Access must be checked on every protected server operation, not just at sign-in.

RULE 8 — Separate authentication journeys
Passenger and driver sign-in/registration should be clearly separated where their requirements differ. Operations accounts use their own authorized workflow. Never offer the Silent Owner as a public login option.

RULE 9 — Identity and phone verification
Passenger and driver accounts require verified phone contact and required identity checks. The exact authentication provider and any SMS/OTP provider must be selected after checking availability, cost, reliability, and security.

RULE 10 — Driver application and approval
A driver must submit required identity and vehicle information and remain pending until an authorized owner or a manager with explicit approval permission approves the application. A driver cannot start operating merely because a form was submitted.

RULE 11 — Required driver/vehicle documents
The planned driver application includes CNIC front/back, selfie, vehicle registration/plate, vehicle photos, vehicle specifications, and proposed routes. Uploads must use protected storage and must not be publicly accessible by predictable URLs.

RULE 12 — One driver, one car for now
Each driver account is linked to one registered car in the current design. Do not build multiple-vehicle selection for a driver unless the user explicitly changes this rule later.

RULE 13 — Correct standard car seat layout
A standard car contains five total seats: one driver seat, one front passenger seat, and three rear passenger seats. Passenger booking excludes the driver's seat. Seat diagrams and capacity calculations must match the actual registered vehicle.

RULE 14 — Vehicle-specific seat layouts
Vans, Coasters, and buses require layouts and capacities appropriate to their vehicle type. The UI must not reuse a sedan layout for a different vehicle. Final seat maps should be verified per vehicle configuration.

RULE 15 — Scheduled rides and route search
Passengers search scheduled rides by origin, destination, date/time, intermediate stops, vehicle type, and available seats. Results should show the approved route, departure/arrival estimate, vehicle, driver verification status, available seats, and transparent fare components.

RULE 16 — Route network and corridors
Initial planned geography includes Khuzdar, Quetta, Karachi, Larkana, Noshki, Gwadar, Turbat, Chaman, Hub, Kalat, Surab, Bela and other approved destinations. Important corridors include N-25/RCD, M-8, N-70 and N-40 where applicable. Routes must be configured operationally, not assumed from a static marketing list.

RULE 17 — Stops, pickup and drop-off
A trip may have approved intermediate stops. Passengers may choose pickup and drop-off points along the approved route. The system must validate that pickup occurs before drop-off and that the selected segment is served by the trip.

RULE 18 — Segment-based seat inventory
A seat can be booked by different passengers for non-overlapping route segments of the same trip. A booking must block the seat only over its reserved segment, while preventing overlapping reservations and impossible timings.

RULE 19 — Segment fares and transparent price
Fares may differ by route segment, seat tier, vehicle, or approved service. Show the fare before confirmation and preserve a fare snapshot on the booking so later price changes do not silently alter a confirmed passenger's terms.

RULE 20 — Front/rear seat tiers
Where configured, front passenger and rear passenger seats can have different fares (as illustrated by the design reference). The fare difference must be shown clearly before the passenger selects/commits to a seat.

RULE 21 — Atomic 10-minute seat hold
During checkout, a selected seat/segment is held for 10 minutes. The hold must be atomic/server-controlled and automatically expire if not completed. Concurrent passengers must not both successfully reserve the same seat segment.

RULE 22 — Charter booking
A passenger may reserve the entire car for a selected route segment. Chartering blocks individual passenger bookings on the same overlapping segment. The app should show the total charter price and make the scope of the reservation clear.

RULE 23 — Seat map clarity
Seat maps must distinguish available, held, booked, unavailable, and selected seats with clear labels and accessible contrast. Never rely on color alone. The map must correspond to the actual vehicle and current validated inventory.

RULE 24 — Female passenger preference safeguard
The planned system may mark adjacent empty seats as “Female Preferred” when a solo female passenger is booked, and show a courteous prompt to a male passenger attempting an adjacent seat, except for passengers booked together in the same group transaction. Exact adjacency, privacy, and policy details require careful validation before implementation; do not expose passenger identity to explain the restriction.

RULE 25 — Booking confirmation and digital ticket
A booking is confirmed only after server validation of seat availability and the required booking/payment conditions. Provide a digital ticket with booking reference, route/segment, passenger/seat details, fare breakdown, QR code, and clear status.

RULE 26 — QR ticket validation
QR codes should identify a booking securely without embedding unnecessary private data. Scanning must validate the ticket server-side when online. Offline scanning may use securely issued data and must synchronize/resolve conflicts when connectivity returns.

RULE 27 — Luggage allowance and heavy luggage
One standard bag is included in the planned fare. Each extra heavy-cargo bag is planned at PKR 500, subject to an authorized/configurable policy. The fee must be visible before booking and applied consistently.

RULE 28 — Luggage tags
Each tagged bag receives a unique four-digit tag ID and QR/barcode on the ticket stub. Prevent duplicate active tags and do not expose passenger CNIC, phone, or other private information through the tag code.

RULE 29 — Terminal and toll fee line items
Show terminal tax/fees and toll fees as separate line items, distinct from the base driver fare. Fee allocation by segment/route must be explicitly configured; never hide them inside an unexplained total.

RULE 30 — Payments are planned, not assumed complete
Payment options may include JazzCash and Easypaisa using authorized account details, with secure transaction reconciliation. Payment integrations are later-phase work and may require provider approval or fees. Never display a payment as successful based only on a client-side button click.

RULE 31 — Cancellation and refund policy
Within the first 8 hours after booking confirmation, cancellation receives a 100% refund. After the first 8 hours, refund is 75% and 25% is retained. Exactly 8 hours falls in the latter category. The elapsed clock begins at booking confirmation, not departure time. Preserve transaction history and use adjustment transactions rather than silently editing financial records.

RULE 32 — Passenger no-show
Passengers should arrive at pickup at least 30 minutes before departure. If a passenger misses departure for reasons within their control, mark No-show and do not automatically refund. If the company caused the problem through delay or incorrect information, protect the passenger and review replacement/full refund options.

RULE 33 — Driver cancellation and replacement
When a driver cancels, require a reason and notify authorized operations to find a replacement driver/vehicle. Preserve passenger bookings where possible. If no suitable replacement exists, refund affected passengers 100%. The company—not passengers—covers additional replacement costs. Track repeat cancellations for human review; do not automatically punish genuine emergencies.

RULE 34 — Delays and passenger communication
Notify passengers promptly when a trip is delayed, include the reason and revised ETA as soon as known, and keep the status current. Do not fabricate an ETA when it is unknown.

RULE 35 — Optional home pickup
Home pickup may be offered for car bookings when the passenger chooses it; it is not automatically offered for every vehicle type. Configure the fee by route/service area and show it upfront. If a driver cannot reach the location, contact the passenger and operations; offer a safe alternative only with passenger agreement, without extra charge. If no reasonable alternative exists, give a full refund.

RULE 36 — Changes to pickup/drop-off after booking
Allow a passenger to request a pickup/drop-off change only if route, timing, and capacity permit. Recalculate the fare and obtain the passenger's approval for additional charges. If the new fare is lower, handle the difference through an authorized adjustment/refund policy. If the change is impossible, leave the original booking unchanged.

RULE 37 — Multi-stop charter and waiting
Whole-car charter may support multiple stops within the approved route/service area. Show itinerary, total price, and any planned waiting allowance/fee upfront. Do not add unapproved waiting fees during a journey; obtain approval before charging for additional waiting.

RULE 38 — Contacting the assigned driver
After confirmation, passengers can contact the assigned driver through an in-app call or privacy-preserving contact option where available. Avoid exposing personal phone numbers unnecessarily. If the driver is replaced, revoke the old contact access and update the passenger's assigned contact.

RULE 39 — Family trip-sharing link
Passengers can optionally share a secure trip-tracking link with family. It expires after the trip or the authorized period, exposes only necessary information, and never includes CNIC or private documents. Clearly indicate when GPS is unavailable or stale.

RULE 40 — Passenger and driver SOS
Provide a one-tap SOS during an active trip for both passengers and drivers. Share the latest GPS location, timestamp, trip/vehicle context, and alert to authorized operations. Show honest pending/delivered/failed status. SOS does not guarantee that public emergency services have been contacted.

RULE 41 — SOS acknowledgement and escalation
An authorized operational recipient should acknowledge SOS. If no acknowledgement arrives within 60 seconds, escalate to other authorized owners/managers. Keep the incident open until resolved, retry delivery where possible, and retain a protected incident history. Configure and verify local emergency telephone numbers before launch; a direct call option should work independently of OTS backend where cellular service is available.

RULE 42 — Offline SOS and breakdown
Make SOS accessible offline, queue and retry the alert when possible, show that it is not yet delivered, and include the latest GPS timestamp. If no working communication channel exists, immediate delivery cannot be guaranteed. Drivers also have a separate breakdown report with location, trip, and problem description; operations should arrange replacement and notify passengers without extra charges. If anyone is in danger, use SOS as well.

RULE 43 — Ratings, appeals, and fair review
After a completed trip, passengers may rate the driver and optionally provide feedback. Drivers may privately rate passengers and report no-show, abuse, or unsafe conduct. Ratings are tied to verified trips. A low rating alone must not automatically suspend an account. Serious complaints require human review; passengers and drivers can appeal. Preserve the original report, appeal, evidence, and recorded outcome; do not treat an allegation as automatic proof of guilt.

5. RULES 44–100 — SECURITY, OPERATIONS, AND SYSTEM BEHAVIOR
====================================================================

RULE 44 — GPS retention and review
Keep detailed ordinary trip GPS history for 90 days. Retain SOS/breakdown GPS records for one year, then review their necessity. Hold investigation-related GPS for as long as genuinely needed with periodic review. Financial records follow applicable legal/accounting requirements. Avoid indefinite retention without a valid reason.

RULE 45 — Silent Owner can inspect critical system data
The Silent Owner may access active live GPS, trip-location history, identity documents, financial data, and other protected operational records needed for system administration, safety, and incident response. Access is private, strongly authenticated, and unavailable to operational users. The authority must not be exposed through operational UI or reports.

RULE 46 — Silent Owner booking override
The Silent Owner may cancel or modify any confirmed booking, adjust fare, initiate a refund, or release a seat when necessary. Never erase the original booking or financial history. Preserve the original state and record corrections as adjustment transactions with a private protected audit trail.

RULE 47 — System-wide configuration
The Silent Owner can configure system-wide rules including refunds, luggage fees, seat holds, routes/fares/taxes, verification, notifications, and security/permissions. Changes apply to future bookings by default. Existing confirmed bookings retain their confirmed price and terms unless a lawful, authorized correction is required and clearly recorded.

RULE 48 — Restore recoverable records
The Silent Owner may restore deleted or corrupted records where valid backups or other recovery mechanisms make this possible. Restore procedures must preserve financial integrity and avoid duplicate bookings/payments. Record recovery privately. If every backup is destroyed or corrupted, recovery cannot be guaranteed.

RULE 49 — Emergency freeze authority
The Silent Owner can activate a one-tap emergency freeze without confirmation. Backend enforcement starts immediately. Status must truthfully indicate pending, partially enforced, or fully enforced; never claim full enforcement before systems confirm it.

RULE 50 — Emergency freeze behavior
Emergency freeze blocks new bookings, trip publishing, and routine activity. Preserve existing tickets and journeys already in progress, and preserve SOS/essential safety functions wherever technically possible. Safety-critical operations should be resilient to ordinary booking restrictions.

RULE 51 — Only Silent Owner lifts emergency freeze
Only the Silent Owner can lift the Silent Owner's emergency freeze. An operational owner or manager cannot bypass it by lifting an ordinary freeze, toggling maintenance, changing permissions, or using a client-side workaround.

RULE 52 — Ordinary operational freeze
Operational owners can activate an ordinary operational freeze. A manager may also activate it after notifying all three operational owners; the manager does not need to wait for their replies before addressing an urgent operational risk. Notification failure must be recorded and retried, but must not block urgent freezing.

RULE 53 — Ordinary unfreeze
Any operational owner or manager may lift an ordinary operational freeze. This does not override or remove a Silent Owner emergency freeze. The backend must evaluate both freeze states independently and apply the stricter active state.

RULE 54 — Maintenance mode belongs to operational owners
Maintenance mode is controlled only from the three operational owners' accounts, not from the Silent Owner website. Any one operational owner may activate, schedule, change, end, or cancel maintenance independently. Managers/workers may submit a maintenance request or report an issue but cannot control the mode.

RULE 55 — Selective maintenance and safety
Maintenance can disable selected features rather than blindly disabling the entire service. Preserve SOS and essential safety functions wherever technically possible. Passenger/driver notices should describe affected features and an ETA if known, without disclosing internal security details.

RULE 56 — Silent Owner system-health dashboard
The private website should show backend/database/storage status, backup health, notification systems, app health, freeze states, and monitoring gaps. Health indicators must reflect observed state and clearly distinguish unknown/unavailable from healthy.

RULE 57 — Private security alerts
The Silent Owner receives private alerts for failed logins, suspicious privileged activity, unauthorized protected-access attempts, backup/export failures, and changes to security settings or recovery credentials. These alerts must not be exposed in operational notifications or logs.

RULE 58 — Silent Owner website outage
If the private website becomes unavailable, backend security and current freeze enforcement must continue. The outage must not unlock access or disable backend rules. Apps may continue authorized operations unless another security or freeze rule applies.

RULE 59 — Fresh authentication for high-risk actions
Require fresh authentication for high-risk actions such as permanent destruction, recovery-credential changes, lifting emergency freeze, and other dangerous operations. A long-lived session alone should not authorize destructive or recovery-sensitive actions.

RULE 60 — Separate 24-character confirmation key
For designated destructive actions, use a separate 24-character confirmation key in addition to normal authentication. Store/verify it securely; never reveal it in logs, UI diagnostics, or ordinary operational records. Do not confuse it with a password or ordinary OTP.

RULE 61 — Safe database deletion flow
Database deletion should begin with one-tap initiation, then show a warning and preview of what will be deleted, related backup copies, and recoverability. The user then enters the 24-character key; the backend verifies the authorization and key before deletion begins. The preview must identify scope clearly enough to prevent accidental destruction.

RULE 62 — Panic lock is separate
A panic lock remains a separate immediate action from destructive deletion. Do not add the deletion preview/key flow to the panic lock if that would delay emergency protection.

RULE 63 — Secure initial recovery setup
Configure a secure Silent Owner recovery method during initial setup. Do not provide an ordinary public “Forgot password?” flow for the Silent Owner. Recovery must never give operational owners access to the Silent Owner account.

RULE 64 — Operational invisibility, private auditability
The Silent Owner's identity, login, recovery, administrative actions, and controls must be invisible to operational users and omitted from operational audit/reporting. However, protected private security records may exist for the Silent Owner and must be accessible only through the private website or authorized recovery mechanism. Invisibility to business operators does not mean no security record exists.

RULE 65 — Encrypted disaster-recovery backups
The Silent Owner can export complete encrypted backups covering the database and essential stored files. Use a separate encryption key, integrity verification, and protected access. Backups must be usable for disaster recovery rather than being an unverified archive.

RULE 66 — Backup retention schedule
Retain daily backups for 30 days, weekly backups for 12 weeks, and monthly backups for 12 months. Retain critical financial/security records according to applicable legal requirements. Backups must be encrypted, access-controlled, and separated from the live database. Retention policy must be technically enforced and monitored.

RULE 67 — Permanent destruction must include backup scope
Before permanent destruction, separately identify whether live data, snapshots, exports, and backup copies are included. Warn that destroying all valid copies can make recovery impossible. Preserve legally required records where applicable and require the designated high-risk authentication/key flow.

RULE 68 — Financial integrity during restore or correction
Never rewrite financial history to make a correction look as if it always existed. Use adjustment/reversal transactions, link them to the original transaction, and preserve who authorized the correction and when. Restores must avoid duplicate charges, refunds, or bookings.

RULE 69 — Live GPS and identity access restrictions
Live GPS, trip history, CNIC/identity documents, financial records, and SOS details are sensitive. Access is limited to roles and operational purposes that require it, with stronger safeguards than ordinary profile data. The Silent Owner has the specified system authority, but that authority is private and protected.

RULE 70 — Silent Owner can change protected system rules
The Silent Owner can change system-wide configuration and permissions. Changes must be backend-enforced, validated, and applied predictably. Existing confirmed prices and terms are not silently altered; use the established booking snapshot and adjustment rules.

RULE 71 — Operational owners' authority
The three operational owners have broad operational authority over drivers/documents, routes/stops/schedules/fares, bookings, cancellations/refunds, finances, workers, and operational responses. Any one owner can make ordinary business decisions unless a stricter rule applies. All three can see each other's operational audit history, but cannot access the Silent Owner's private controls or audit records.

RULE 72 — Unanimous operational-owner replacement
Creating or replacing an operational owner requires each of the three existing operational owners' individual OTP approvals. There is no two-of-three bypass. If one is unavailable, the request remains blocked. The Silent Owner may independently add, remove, freeze, or replace operational owners.

RULE 73 — Owner OTP security
Each approval OTP must be short-lived, single-use, and bound to the exact owner-creation/replacement request. Do not accept a code from another request or reuse it after completion. Record the operational approval outcome without exposing Silent Owner identity.

RULE 74 — Owner self-service password recovery
An operational owner may recover their own account through SMS OTP to their verified phone. This does not permit recovery of another owner's account or Silent Owner access. Changing a phone number requires current authentication and OTP to the new number. Losing both the account and old phone requires a separate recovery process.

RULE 75 — Worker account management
Operational owners can create, change, suspend, and remove workers. A manager may do so only if an owner explicitly grants worker-management permission. Each worker gets an individual account, assigned role, and terminal/work assignment; shared logins are not allowed.

RULE 76 — Worker suspension and reactivation
Suspending a worker revokes access and active sessions where possible while preserving their work history. An owner or properly authorized manager may reactivate the worker. Keep an audit trail of who changed the account status and why.

RULE 77 — Manager role and assigned permissions
Managers are a planned operational role with individually assigned powers such as refunds, driver approval/rejection, major route/fare changes, route/stop/schedule management, staff/terminal supervision, and breakdown/reassignment work. Do not assume every manager automatically receives every permission.

RULE 78 — Manager privilege boundaries
Managers cannot change each other's permissions, appoint/remove/promote managers, or grant themselves privileges. Only operational owners can create, remove, suspend, or change manager permissions. Managers can see each other's operational work/actions as allowed by the operations design.

RULE 79 — Reversal of manager decisions
Manager B may reverse Manager A's decision only if Manager B has permission for that action. Preserve the original decision and record the reversal and reason. Financial corrections must use compensating transactions rather than silently overwriting history.

RULE 80 — Manager suspension and worker limits
Any operational owner can suspend/reactivate a manager. Managers cannot manage other manager accounts. Worker management is allowed only when an owner has explicitly granted that permission. No operational role may access Silent Owner controls.

RULE 81 — Worker permissions
Workers may sell walk-in cash tickets, search bookings by CNIC/phone/ticket ID when necessary for the task, print/attach luggage tags, scan tickets, support boarding/luggage handover, and print/export authorized checkpoint manifests. They cannot approve/reject drivers, change/approve fares, issue refunds, manage owner accounts, freeze/unfreeze apps, or access Silent Owner controls.

RULE 82 — Worker-visible data boundaries
Workers see the information required for their current task and operational instructions, not complete financial data or manager audit histories. Search by sensitive identifiers must be restricted, justified, and logged appropriately. Do not expose unnecessary CNIC details on manifests or tickets.

RULE 83 — Offline counter ticketing
Workers may prepare cash-ticket sales offline, but tickets remain pending and unconfirmed until the server validates availability after reconnection. Use a secure pending queue, idempotency, duplicate prevention, and a conflict flag for staff resolution. Reconcile collected cash with validated sales.

RULE 84 — Account restoration authority
A worker may be restored by an owner or authorized manager; a manager may be restored only by an operational owner; an operational owner blocked by the Silent Owner can be restored only by the Silent Owner. Passenger/driver restoration follows owner/manager permissions unless a Silent Owner restriction applies. Only the Silent Owner lifts its own emergency freeze.

RULE 85 — Driver approval and one-car registration
Driver onboarding requires the defined identity/vehicle documents and routes. The application stays pending until an owner or manager with explicit permission approves it. One driver is registered to one car for now. No unapproved driver can publish or operate trips.

RULE 86 — Trip and seat operations
Drivers can publish approved trips, manage available seats, view the passenger manifest, and manage the active journey within authorized limits. The seat map follows the actual vehicle layout and segment inventory rules. A trip/seat action cannot bypass server validation or freeze/security rules.

RULE 87 — Driver availability states
Drivers can set Available (searchable) or On Leave/Paused (hidden) as permitted. Busy/On Route is automatically active for a current journey where possible, with route duration and status based on the trip. Driver status must not imply availability for a trip they cannot serve.

RULE 88 — Driver breakdown and passenger reassignment
A driver can report a breakdown with trip, location, and problem details. Authorized managers receive the alert and can arrange a suitable replacement, validate capacity/route, preserve bookings/payments, update seats/luggage tags, and notify passengers. No extra charge is passed to passengers. If no suitable replacement exists, contact passengers for resolution/refund.

RULE 89 — Passenger/driver ratings and complaints
Ratings are allowed only after completed, verified trips. Passenger feedback is optional. Driver ratings of passengers remain private. Low ratings alone do not trigger automatic suspension; serious allegations need fair human review. Both sides can appeal, and original records/outcomes remain preserved.

RULE 90 — SOS incident lifecycle
SOS is not a routine notification. It must have delivery state, retries where possible, authorized acknowledgement, escalation to other authorized owners/managers after 60 seconds without acknowledgement, and a resolution lifecycle. Direct emergency calling should be independently available where cellular service permits. Never promise successful delivery if the device has no working communications.

RULE 91 — English and Urdu language support
Support English and Urdu across both apps and the Silent Owner website where appropriate. Urdu must use correct right-to-left layout and shaping. Important notices, tickets, and SOS information should be bilingual. Keep the structure extensible for future languages without requiring a redesign.

RULE 92 — First-launch language selection
On the first launch of either app, show a simple choice between English and Urdu (اردو). Save the choice and remember it on later launches. Do not ask again unless the user changes language in Settings. Changing language must not erase data. Apply the selected language throughout the user's experience where appropriate, including correct Urdu RTL layout.

RULE 93 — App updates and security
Optional updates show a notice but allow continued use. Require a mandatory update when necessary for a critical security fix or essential compatibility. Explain why the update is required. Preserve bookings, tickets, account information, and other user data. Keep SOS and essential safety functions available wherever technically possible during update restrictions. If an old version is unsafe/incompatible, restrict affected features rather than falsely claiming it is secure.

RULE 94 — Internet connection problems and offline operation
Clearly show Online, Offline, or Reconnecting. Cache only information needed for approved offline tasks, protect it, and remove it when no longer needed. Securely queue supported actions and synchronize when connection returns. Never confirm a booking offline without server validation. Prevent duplicate bookings/tickets/payments/alerts after reconnection and flag conflicts for authorized resolution. SOS should show delivered, pending, or failed honestly and retry where possible.

RULE 95 — Notifications and notification permissions
Keep important updates available in-app, with push notifications for timely events and SMS/WhatsApp fallbacks where configured, available, and permitted. Prioritize booking confirmations, driver/vehicle changes, delays, cancellations, breakdowns, and SOS. Track sent/delivered where supported/failed/retrying states without falsely claiming delivery. If permission is denied, explain how to enable it and provide another way to check updates. Hide sensitive details from lock screens where appropriate. SOS follows its own acknowledgement/escalation rules and must not depend only on ordinary notification preferences.

RULE 96 — Account and device security
Users can review active sessions and registered devices and revoke a lost/stolen device's sessions from another authorized device or secure recovery process. Use the approved authentication method and verified phone where required. Sensitive account changes require fresh authentication. Password changes, suspension, and security incidents revoke affected sessions wherever technically possible. Never expose authentication secrets or sensitive session data to other users. Keep appropriate security events in protected logs without exposing Silent Owner identity/actions to operational users.

RULE 97 — Privacy and personal-data access
Collect only personal data needed for safe, lawful operations. Apply least-privilege access and allow users to request access to/correction of their own profile data subject to law. CNIC documents, live GPS, financial details, and SOS records require stricter controls. Follow defined GPS retention and legal requirements. Honor valid deletion requests while retaining records required for law, accounting, active disputes, or safety investigations. Silent Owner identity, controls, and private security records remain inaccessible to operational users. Record authorized sensitive-data access in the appropriate protected audit trail.

RULE 98 — Fraud detection and suspicious activity
Monitor repeated failed logins, unusual booking patterns, duplicate payment attempts, duplicate tickets, and suspicious account creation. Apply proportionate protections such as rate limits, temporary restrictions, or additional verification. Unusual activity alone is not proof of fraud and should not cause unjustified permanent bans. Escalate serious incidents to authorized operational personnel or Silent Owner private security alerts according to incident type. Preserve evidence securely and provide an appropriate review/appeal path for affected users.

RULE 99 — Customer support and complaints
Provide passengers and drivers an in-app support section for booking problems, payment disputes, misconduct, lost luggage, and general complaints. Give every case a unique reference number, status, assigned authorized reviewer, and recorded resolution. Safety complaints receive urgent priority and escalation. Users can follow up on their own cases without seeing another person's confidential data. Preserve relevant evidence and allow review where appropriate. Never expose Silent Owner private records.

RULE 100 — Full requirements audit before implementation
Before implementation proceeds, review the consolidated specification for contradictions, missing decisions, security gaps, technical limitations, operational edge cases, accessibility, privacy, legal requirements, and costs that may exceed the PKR 0 starting budget. Clearly distinguish confirmed requirements, provisional design choices, unresolved decisions, and features that require paid providers. The user asked for one question at a time. This is a planning checkpoint, not authorization to start coding or change other resources.

4. CONFIRMED FOLLOW-UP DECISIONS (2026-10-24)

These decisions were explicitly confirmed by the user during the follow-up requirements review. They supplement the existing product rules 1–100 and conversation rules 1–45; they do not renumber or replace those sequences.

FD-01 — Actual vehicle seat layouts
For hi-roof vans, Coasters, and buses, use the actual seat layout of each registered vehicle. The layout must be configured and approved by an operational owner and used for seat selection, availability, and bookings. Cars remain fixed at 5 total seats: 1 driver seat, 1 front passenger seat, and 3 rear passenger seats.

FD-02 — Female-seat courtesy preference
When a woman books a seat alone, mark an adjacent available passenger seat as “Female Preferred.” If a man attempts to book that adjacent seat, show a polite courtesy warning. This warning alone must not automatically block the booking.

FD-03 — Terminal and toll fee transparency
Calculate terminal fees and toll charges separately for each route or route segment, display them as separate line items before booking, and save a fare snapshot with the confirmed booking. Later price changes must not unexpectedly change the price or terms of an existing confirmed booking.

FD-04 — Changing pickup or drop-off after booking
If a passenger requests a different pickup or drop-off:
- If the new fare is higher, display the extra charge and obtain the passenger’s approval before collecting payment.
- If the new fare is lower, handle the difference through an authorized refund or fare adjustment.
- If the change is not possible because of route, timing, or seat availability, leave the original booking unchanged.
- Preserve the original booking and fare history for auditability.


FD-05 — Vehicle-specific included luggage allowance
The included luggage allowance differs by vehicle type:
- Cars: 1 standard bag is included in the ticket price.
- Hi-roof vans, Coasters, and buses: 3 luggage items are included free.
- Additional heavy luggage costs PKR 500 per additional bag, subject to the luggage rules and capacity limits for that vehicle.
- Show applicable luggage charges clearly before the passenger confirms the booking.
- Each checked bag receives a unique 4-digit tag ID and QR/barcode linked to the ticket. The tag must not expose the passenger’s private information.

FD-06 — Cancellation refund policy applies to all passenger bookings
The standard passenger cancellation refund policy applies to all passenger booking types, including private whole-car bookings, unless an authorized operational owner approves an exception:
- If the passenger cancels within the first 8 hours after booking confirmation, the standard refund is 100%.
- After the first 8 hours have elapsed, the standard refund is 75%, with 25% retained.
- Exactly 8 hours after booking confirmation is treated as “after the first 8 hours” for this policy, so the standard refund is 75%.
- The time calculation starts from booking confirmation, not from the scheduled departure time.
- Any authorized exception must be recorded with the decision, reason, responsible authorized person, and financial adjustment history. Preserve the original booking, cancellation, and refund records.

FD-07 — Passenger may choose an immediate full refund after driver cancellation
When a driver cancels a confirmed trip, the company should try to arrange a suitable replacement driver and/or vehicle without charging passengers extra. Once the passenger is informed of the cancellation, the passenger may choose a 100% refund immediately instead of waiting for a replacement. If no suitable replacement is available, the passenger receives a 100% refund. Preserve the original booking and cancellation history, record the passenger's choice, and reconcile any financial adjustment without altering the original transaction record.

FD-08 — Automatically release expired seat holds
When a passenger selects a seat and starts checkout, the system may place a temporary 10-minute hold on that seat. If booking confirmation/payment is not completed before the hold expires, the backend automatically releases the seat so other passengers can book it. Hold creation, expiry, release, and confirmation must be enforced atomically by the backend to prevent two passengers from confirming the same seat for overlapping trip segments. An expired hold must not be treated as a confirmed booking.

FD-09 — Retry payment while a seat hold remains active
If payment fails or the payment provider times out, show the passenger a clear “Retry payment” option while their 10-minute seat hold remains active, along with the remaining hold time. Retrying payment must not silently extend the hold. If the hold expires before a successful, server-verified booking confirmation, release the seat and clearly tell the passenger. Handle delayed or duplicate provider callbacks idempotently so a late payment result cannot create a duplicate booking or reserve an already-released seat; if money is captured after the hold has expired and the booking cannot be honored, flag it for a safe refund/reconciliation process.

FD-10 — Verify uncertain payment status after connectivity loss
If a passenger loses internet during checkout and the payment result is uncertain, show “Payment status checking” or equivalent rather than falsely declaring the payment failed. When connectivity returns, verify the transaction status with the trusted backend/payment provider before deciding whether the booking is confirmed, payment should be retried, or a refund/reconciliation is needed. Do not create duplicate charges or bookings. Keep the seat-hold countdown honest; connectivity loss must not silently extend the hold, and if the hold expires, the backend must not promise that the seat remains reserved. Clearly explain the outcome to the passenger.

FD-11 — Confirm cash bookings only after receipt and server validation
For a terminal or counter sale, the worker may mark cash as received only after actually collecting it and recording the amount and sale details. A booking must not be represented as confirmed merely because a sale was started or cash is expected. If the worker is offline, the sale remains clearly pending until the server verifies seat/segment availability and accepts it on reconnection. Prevent duplicate submissions and flag conflicts for authorized staff resolution; reconcile recorded cash against accepted, cancelled, and rejected sales. Do not falsely promise a seat before server validation.

FD-12 — Unique QR tickets with online and offline validation
Every confirmed booking receives a unique digital QR ticket linked to its booking. Authorized staff scan the QR to validate the ticket. When online, check current booking status with the server. When offline, validate against securely synced, unexpired ticket/manifest data and clearly mark the scan as offline/pending server reconciliation. Protect against copied or reused tickets: record scans and boarding state, prevent the same ticket from being boarded twice, and reconcile offline scans when connectivity returns. A QR code alone is not proof that a booking is currently valid; protect ticket data and do not encode exposed passenger private information in the QR.

FD-13 — Recover tickets on a new phone and revoke other sessions
If a passenger loses or replaces their phone, they can securely sign in on another device and retrieve their confirmed bookings/tickets from the server. Provide a “Log out all other devices” action that revokes other active sessions and credentials where supported, including the lost phone, and record the security event. Require appropriate re-authentication for this sensitive action, notify the passenger through a verified channel where available, and do not delete bookings or tickets when a device session is revoked. QR tickets remain subject to current booking-status and duplicate-boarding checks.

FD-14 — Correct passenger identity details before departure
A passenger may request correction of an incorrectly entered name or CNIC/passenger identity detail before departure. Identity-sensitive changes must be verified and approved by an authorized operational owner or manager who has the relevant permission. Preserve the original value, corrected value, requester, approver, reason, and timestamp in the protected audit history. Do not expose CNIC details unnecessarily in tickets, QR codes, manifests, or notifications. A correction request does not automatically change a confirmed booking until it is approved and recorded.

FD-15 — Privacy-first access to CNIC and identity documents
Privacy is a first-priority requirement. Full CNIC numbers and identity-document images must be visible only to specifically authorized people whose assigned duties require them for identity verification, safety, or a legitimate approved investigation. Do not make these details available by default to every worker, driver, or person handling a booking. Apply least-privilege backend access, mask sensitive fields in ordinary views, avoid placing CNIC/private document data in QR codes, notifications, routine manifests, or logs, and securely record authorized access to protected identity records. Encrypt protected records in transit and at rest where supported, define retention/deletion rules according to applicable law and legitimate safety needs, and never treat hiding a field in the UI alone as sufficient authorization.

FD-16 — Driver identity, licence, and vehicle verification before approval
Before a driver can publish trips or accept passengers, require submission and verification of the driver's CNIC, selfie, valid driving licence, and required registered-vehicle documents and photographs. The driver remains in a clearly labelled Pending Verification/Approval state until an operational owner or a manager with explicit driver-approval permission reviews and approves the application. Rejections or requests for more information must give an appropriate reason and allow resubmission/appeal under the operational process. Enforce approval on the backend, not only by hiding trip-publishing controls in the app. Restrict access to identity documents using FD-15's privacy-first rules and preserve a protected review/audit record.

FD-17 — Expiring documents: staged reminders and temporary restriction
Track the expiry date of every required driver licence and vehicle document. Send the driver reminders one month before expiry, then again two weeks before, one week before, three days before, and one day before the expiry date. Make the affected document and its expiry date clear in each reminder, and provide a straightforward way to submit the renewed document. Once a required document expires, temporarily block the driver from publishing new trips and accepting new bookings until the renewed document has been submitted and verified by an authorized operational owner or a manager explicitly permitted to perform that verification. Do not treat uploading a replacement as automatic approval. Notify the driver of the restriction and what is needed to restore eligibility; notify the authorized operations team so they can follow up. Keep notification attempts/failures and verification decisions in the audit history, and enforce the restriction on the backend. Preserve existing confirmed journeys and tickets while the case is handled, with authorized staff able to arrange any necessary safety-related follow-up.

FD-18 — Temporary driver suspension for serious safety complaints
When a serious passenger safety or misconduct complaint is reported, an operational owner or a manager with explicit permission may temporarily suspend the implicated driver while the complaint is reviewed. During suspension, enforce a backend restriction preventing the driver from publishing new trips or accepting new bookings. Notify the driver appropriately, explain the review process without exposing a complainant's private information unnecessarily, and provide a suitable opportunity to respond and appeal. Authorized staff must review the available evidence and record the complaint, suspension reason, decision-maker, timestamps, investigation outcome, and any reinstatement or further action in protected audit history. A complaint or low rating alone must not silently become a permanent finding; handle the matter proportionately, with urgent safety protection where warranted and privacy safeguards for all parties. Preserve existing confirmed journeys and arrange safe operational follow-up where needed.

FD-19 — Suspicious passenger account activity and temporary lock
Monitor for suspicious passenger-account activity, including repeated failed login attempts and unusual attempts to access protected information. When risk thresholds or security rules indicate likely abuse, temporarily lock or restrict the affected account and revoke risky active sessions where supported. Notify the passenger through a verified contact method where available, without exposing sensitive account details. Require appropriate identity/account verification before restoring restricted access, with a safe recovery route for legitimate users and a way to request review if they believe the lock is mistaken. Enforce restrictions on the backend, rate-limit repeated attempts, and preserve security-event timestamps and outcomes in protected audit records. Do not expose private data or reveal security mechanisms that would help an attacker bypass protections. Preserve confirmed booking and ticket records; handle urgent journey and safety needs through authorized support.

FD-20 — Suspicious driver account activity and temporary restriction
Apply the suspicious-activity protections in FD-19 to driver accounts as well. If repeated failed logins, unusual protected-data access, or other security signals indicate likely account compromise, temporarily lock or restrict the account, revoke risky active sessions where supported, and notify the driver through a verified contact method where available. Until the account's security is verified, backend rules must prevent the affected account from publishing new trips or accepting new bookings. Require appropriate account/identity verification before restoring these capabilities, provide a safe recovery and review route for legitimate drivers, and preserve confirmed trip records and tickets. Notify authorized operations where needed for safe handling of affected trips, and record security events, restriction decisions, verification, and restoration in protected audit history. Do not expose sensitive details or security mechanisms that could help attackers bypass controls.

FD-21 — Optional emergency contacts and consent-based trip sharing
Allow a passenger to optionally add an emergency contact. The passenger may choose to share relevant trip details or a secure trip-tracking link with that contact, especially when seeking help during an SOS incident. Sharing must be consent-based and show the passenger what will be shared and with whom. Use a secure, time-limited link that expires after the trip or the authorized sharing period, disclose only the minimum necessary trip information, and never include the passenger's CNIC or other unnecessary sensitive data. Make clear when location is stale or unavailable, and allow the passenger to stop sharing where appropriate. Emergency workflows should use the configured contact only according to the passenger's choice and applicable safety rules; do not imply that an emergency contact or public emergency service has been reached unless delivery is confirmed. Record relevant sharing/delivery status without unnecessarily exposing private details.
